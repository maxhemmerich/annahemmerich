About

Painting in acrylic, oil and collage, on canvas, panel and cut wood.

Each painting starts from a drawn structure — a net, a fence, a grid, a scaffold. Then the surface is covered over: flat blocks of paint, pasted paper, sawn pieces of wood, and outlines laid on last and left to wobble. The drawing underneath stays half visible.

The palette is mixed to stay saturated and mostly unmixed — chartreuse, vermilion, cobalt, ochre, hot pink. The structure holds the picture together while everything else is stacked on top of it.

Titles point somewhere — Tennis, Huckleberry bush, Garbage Collector — and the painting rarely follows all the way.

I grew up in a small town on Vancouver Island, in Canada, spending a lot of time outside on my own. At sixteen a friend hired me as her studio assistant and let me paint in her studio — that is where I understood what an art community is, and that this might be something I could do for a living. I live in Leipzig now.

The work is half collage and half paint, and mostly intuitive: I might find a piece of paper that gives me an idea, or a wall with a shelf on it, and draw it fifteen minutes before I start painting. I want it to stay decorative — I get frustrated by paintings that tell you how to feel. The old paper and printed matter I pick up keep the colour in check, and I have shown paintings standing on shelves I made.
